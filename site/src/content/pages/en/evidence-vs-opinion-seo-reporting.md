SEO reporting contains both evidence and opinion. That is not a flaw: observed facts need interpretation before they can support a decision. The problem begins when a report presents interpretation as fact, or displays metrics without explaining how they relate to the recommended work.

A useful report labels the difference. Evidence describes what was observed, where it came from and when it was collected. Opinion explains what the author thinks the observation means. A recommendation then joins the two with explicit reasoning, scope and a proposed check. Readers can challenge any part without dismissing the whole report.

This distinction improves decisions under uncertainty. It does not turn SEO into a laboratory experiment or remove professional judgement. It gives judgement a visible foundation and lets the team update its view when later evidence disagrees.
